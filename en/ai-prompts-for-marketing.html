How to Write AI Prompts for Marketing That Actually Work

November 13, 20268 min readAI tools

AI assistants like ChatGPT and Claude can write ad copy, plan content, draft emails and brainstorm offers in seconds. Yet many business owners try them once, get a bland, generic answer, and decide the tools are overrated. In most cases the problem is not the tool; it is the prompt. A vague request produces a vague answer.

This guide gives you a simple formula for writing marketing prompts that produce useful, specific results, with examples you can copy and adapt today.

Why most marketing prompts fail

Compare these two requests:

  • Weak: "Write an Instagram ad for my shop."
  • Strong: "Act as a direct-response copywriter. I sell handmade candles priced at 120 AED to women aged 25 to 40 in Dubai who buy gifts for friends. Write five Instagram ad versions under 40 words, each with a different angle (gift, relaxation, home decor, limited batch, seasonal), and end each with a call to order on WhatsApp. Avoid exaggerated claims."

The second prompt tells the AI who to be, what to do, who the customer is, what format to use and what to avoid. The difference in output quality is dramatic, and it costs you only one extra minute.

The five-part prompt formula

PartQuestion it answersExample
1. RoleWho should the AI act as?"Act as an email marketer for e-commerce brands."
2. TaskWhat exactly should it do?"Write a three-email welcome sequence."
3. ContextWhat does it need to know?Product, price, audience, location, offer, tone.
4. FormatHow should the answer look?"A table", "five versions", "under 60 words each".
5. ConstraintsWhat must it avoid?"No invented statistics", "no slang", "in Gulf Arabic".

You do not need all five every time, but the more of them you include, the less editing you will do afterwards.

Context is your competitive advantage

The AI knows a lot about marketing in general, but nothing about your business. The context you add is what turns a generic answer into one that sounds like your brand. Useful details include:

  • What you sell, the price and the main benefit.
  • Who buys it, where they live and what they worry about before buying.
  • Your tone of voice: formal, friendly, playful, premium.
  • Examples of past posts or emails you liked, pasted directly into the prompt.
  • Any rules: words you never use, claims you cannot make, channels you sell on.

Save this information in a note and paste it at the start of every new conversation. It takes a few seconds and improves every answer.

Ready-to-use prompt templates

Replace everything in [brackets] with your own details.

  1. Ad copy: "Act as a copywriter. I sell [product] at [price] to [audience] in [city]. Write five ad versions under 40 words using different angles: benefit, problem, story, offer and social proof. End each with [call to action]. No exaggerated claims."
  2. Content ideas: "I run a [type of business] account for [audience]. Suggest 30 post ideas split into educational, behind-the-scenes, customer questions and offers. Give each a one-line hook."
  3. Email: "Write a short launch email for [product] to existing customers. Subject line under 45 characters, one clear benefit, one call to action, friendly tone."
  4. Customer research: "List the ten most common objections someone might have before buying [product] at [price], and a short honest answer to each."
  5. Repurposing: "Turn this blog post into one carousel, three short video hooks and one email: [paste text]."

Treat the first answer as a draft

Good results usually come from a short conversation, not a single message. After the first answer, push it further with follow-ups such as:

  • "Make version three shorter and more urgent."
  • "Rewrite this in a warmer, more personal tone."
  • "Give me five more hooks in the style of the second one."
  • "What is weak about this ad, and how would you fix it?"

Asking the AI to critique its own work is surprisingly effective, especially before you spend money on ads.

Writing prompts for Arabic content

If your audience reads Arabic, say so clearly and specify the variety: Modern Standard Arabic for formal content, or a dialect such as Gulf, Egyptian or Levantine for casual social posts. Ask for natural phrasing rather than literal translation, and always have a native speaker review important copy. When you need both languages, ask for each version separately instead of translating one into the other.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Accepting invented facts: AI can produce statistics, reviews or claims that sound real but are not. Check every number and never publish fake testimonials.
  • Sharing sensitive data: do not paste customer personal information, passwords or confidential numbers into a chatbot.
  • Publishing without editing: add your own experience, examples and voice so your content does not sound like everyone else's.
  • Asking for everything at once: split big tasks into steps: research, outline, draft, edit.

Build your own prompt library

Every time a prompt gives you a great result, save it in a document with a short label, such as "Instagram ad, gift angle" or "welcome email". Over a few weeks you will build a personal library that saves hours every month. Frequently asked questions

Is ChatGPT or Claude better for marketing?

Both are capable for most marketing tasks such as copywriting, ideas and planning. The quality of your prompt and the context you give matter more than the specific tool, so try the same prompt in both and keep what works best for you.

Can I use AI-written copy in my ads?

Yes, as long as you review it, make sure every claim is true, and follow the advertising platform's policies. Treat the AI output as a draft that you edit, not as final copy.

How long should a good prompt be?

Long enough to include the role, task, context, format and constraints. For most marketing tasks that means a few sentences. Longer is fine if the extra detail is relevant.

Can AI write good Arabic marketing content?

It can produce useful drafts, especially when you specify the dialect and tone. Always have a native speaker review important copy, because phrasing and cultural nuance matter a lot in Arabic.

Is it safe to paste business information into AI tools?

Avoid sharing personal customer data, passwords or confidential financial details. General information about your product, audience and offers is usually fine, but check the tool's privacy settings and your own company policy.
