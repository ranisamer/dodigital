WhatsApp Business for Online Stores: Turning Chats into Orders

November 19, 20269 min readE-commerce

In much of the Middle East, customers prefer to ask before they buy. They want to know if a size is available, how long delivery takes, or whether a colour looks the same as in the photo, and for many of them the natural place to ask is WhatsApp. For a small online store, this is an opportunity: every chat is a customer who is already interested. The difference between a store that sells through WhatsApp and one that just answers questions is a little preparation and a clear process.

This guide walks through how to set up WhatsApp Business properly and turn conversations into orders, without spamming people or burning out your team.

Set up a professional business profile

WhatsApp Business is a free app designed for small businesses, separate from your personal account. Take a few minutes to complete the profile, because it is the first thing many customers check:

  • Business name and logo: the same name and logo customers see on your store and social media.
  • Short description: what you sell and who it is for, in one or two lines.
  • Hours, location and website: so customers know when to expect a reply and where to order.
  • Catalogue: add your main products with clear photos, prices and short descriptions so customers can browse without asking for everything.

Use a phone number dedicated to the business, so you can share it publicly and hand it to a team member later if needed.

Use the built-in tools

WhatsApp Business includes simple features that save a lot of time:

FeatureHow to use it
Greeting messageA short automatic welcome for new chats, with what you sell and how to order
Away messageAn automatic reply outside working hours saying when you will respond
Quick repliesSaved answers to common questions about price, delivery, payment and returns
LabelsTag chats as new enquiry, pending payment, paid, shipped or follow-up
Catalogue and linksShare a product directly in the chat instead of describing it

Labels are especially useful: at the end of each day you can see who is waiting for a reply, who has not paid yet and who needs a follow-up.

Grow your contact list with consent

Only message people who have contacted you or agreed to receive messages from you. Sending offers to numbers you collected without permission annoys people, can get your number reported or restricted, and goes against WhatsApp's policies. Good ways to invite people include:

  • A clear WhatsApp button on your website and product pages, with a pre-filled message.
  • A link in your Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat bios.
  • A QR code on packaging, receipts and in your shop.
  • Asking customers at checkout whether they would like updates on WhatsApp.

A pre-filled message for each campaign, such as "Hi, I saw your Ramadan box offer", also tells you which ad or post brought the chat in.

A simple sales conversation flow

Most WhatsApp sales follow the same steps. Having a clear flow helps your team reply quickly and consistently:

  1. Welcome and understand: greet the customer by name if you can, and ask one question to understand what they need.
  2. Recommend: suggest one or two products from your catalogue, with photos and prices.
  3. Answer doubts: delivery time, size, returns and payment, using your quick replies.
  4. Make ordering easy: send a payment link or a clear summary of the order with the total.
  5. Confirm: send an order confirmation and expected delivery date.

Reply speed matters. A customer who waits hours for an answer may have already bought elsewhere. If you cannot reply quickly at certain times, say so clearly in your away message.

Payment and order confirmation

The fewer steps between "I want it" and "I paid", the better. Offer the payment methods your customers trust, such as a card payment link, bank transfer or cash on delivery where it makes sense. After payment, send a short confirmation with the order details, delivery estimate and a contact for questions. Keep a simple record of every WhatsApp order in your store system or a spreadsheet, so nothing gets lost in long chat histories.

Follow-ups that don't annoy

Many customers ask, get distracted and forget. A single polite follow-up a day or two later often brings the sale back: "Hi, just checking if you had any other questions about the bag you asked about." If they do not reply, leave it there. After delivery, a short thank-you and a request for a review builds trust for future customers. Broadcasts, groups and channels

Broadcast lists send a message individually to many contacts at once, but only people who have saved your number receive them. Use them sparingly for genuinely useful updates: a restock of a popular product, a seasonal offer, or a new collection. Groups are better for communities where members talk to each other, and channels suit one-way updates to a larger audience. Whatever you choose, keep promotional messages limited and make it easy for people to opt out.

Writing messages that sell

Keep messages short, friendly and specific. Use the language and dialect your customers use, add a clear photo or product link, and end with one simple next step. Frequently asked questions

Is WhatsApp Business free?

The WhatsApp Business app is free to download and use for small businesses. Larger businesses that need automation, many agents or integrations use the WhatsApp Business Platform through an approved provider, which has its own pricing.

Can I message customers who haven't contacted me?

You should only message people who have contacted you or agreed to receive messages. Unsolicited messages annoy people, can lead to reports and restrictions on your number, and go against WhatsApp's policies.

How quickly should I reply?

As quickly as you reasonably can during working hours, ideally within minutes for new enquiries. Set an away message that tells customers when you will reply outside those hours.

Should I use broadcast lists for offers?

Yes, but sparingly. Send only relevant, useful updates to people who have saved your number and agreed to hear from you, and make it easy to opt out.

How do I keep track of orders made on WhatsApp?

Use labels for each order stage and record every confirmed order in your store system or a simple spreadsheet. Relying on chat history alone makes it easy to miss payments and deliveries.
