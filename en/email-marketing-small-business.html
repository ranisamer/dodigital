Email Marketing for Small Businesses in the Middle East: A Beginner's Playbook

November 14, 20269 min readEmail marketing

In much of the Middle East, small businesses live on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp. Those channels are powerful, but you do not own them. An algorithm change can cut your reach overnight, and an account can be restricted without warning. Email is different: your list belongs to you, it travels with you from one platform to the next, and a single message can reach every subscriber who asked to hear from you.

Email is not a replacement for social media or WhatsApp. It is the quiet channel that keeps customers coming back, and it costs very little to start. This playbook walks you through the basics, step by step, with a focus on stores and small brands selling in the Gulf and the wider Arab market.

Why email still matters for small businesses

  • You own the audience. Followers belong to a platform; subscribers are on your own list.
  • It reaches people when they are ready. Customers check their inbox when they have time, and can come back to your message later.
  • It is cheap to run. Most email tools offer a free or low-cost plan for small lists.
  • It works well with your other channels. A product you post on Instagram can be followed by a fuller email with photos, details and a direct link to buy.

Email works especially well for repeat purchases: beauty and fragrance, coffee and food, fashion, digital products and anything customers buy more than once.

Choose a simple tool and set it up properly

You do not need an expensive platform. Look for a tool with a free or affordable starter plan, a drag-and-drop editor, signup forms, basic automation (at least a welcome sequence) and simple reports. Popular options include MailerLite, Brevo, Mailchimp and Klaviyo; some store platforms also include built-in email features. Compare the current plans yourself, because pricing and limits change often.

Before sending your first campaign, take care of three setup steps:

  1. Use an email address on your own domain (for example, hello@yourstore.com) instead of a free webmail address. It looks more professional and improves delivery.
  2. Authenticate your domain. Your email tool will guide you to add SPF, DKIM and DMARC records in your domain settings. This tells inbox providers that your emails are really from you.
  3. Add your business details to the footer: your business name, a way to contact you and an unsubscribe link.

Build your list with clear consent

The golden rule: only email people who agreed to receive your emails. Buying lists or adding every customer without permission damages your sender reputation and may break privacy laws. Data protection rules exist in several countries in the region (for example the UAE and Saudi Arabia), so check the requirements that apply to your business.

Practical ways to grow a list with consent:

  • A signup form on your website with a clear promise: what people will receive and how often.
  • A checkbox at checkout for news and offers, left unticked by default.
  • A link in your Instagram or TikTok bio to a simple signup page.
  • A lead magnet: a free guide, a checklist, a discount on the first order or early access to a sale.
  • In person: at events, pop-ups and in your shop, with a QR code to the signup form.
Tip: the best lead magnet solves one small problem for your ideal customer. A skincare brand could offer a one-page routine guide; a coffee shop could offer a brewing guide; a freelancer could offer a checklist. Keep it short and genuinely useful.

Set up a welcome sequence first

New subscribers are usually at their most interested in the first days after signing up. A welcome sequence is a short series of automatic emails that introduces your brand while that interest is high. A simple version:

EmailWhenPurpose
1. WelcomeImmediatelyDeliver the lead magnet or discount, say what to expect
2. Your storyDay 2Why you started, what makes your products different
3. Best sellersDay 4Show your most popular products and who they suit
4. Help and trustDay 6Answer common questions: delivery, returns, payment
5. Gentle nudgeDay 8Remind them of the welcome offer before it expires

Write it once, switch it on, and it works for every new subscriber.

What to send after that

Once the welcome sequence is running, plan a regular rhythm you can keep, such as one email every one or two weeks. Mix value and selling so people stay subscribed:

  • Helpful content: tips, how-to guides and answers to customer questions.
  • Product news: new arrivals, restocks and behind-the-scenes stories.
  • Seasonal campaigns: Ramadan, Eid, national days, White Friday and back to school are natural moments to email in the region. Plan them weeks ahead.
  • Customer moments: a thank-you after purchase, a reminder to reorder, a note on a customer's birthday if they shared it.

To plan these topics quickly, you can reuse the same system we describe in how to plan a month of social media content, then turn your best posts into emails.

Writing emails that people open and read

A good marketing email is short, clear and has one goal. Before writing, decide the single action you want the reader to take.

  • Subject line: specific and honest. Promise what the email delivers; avoid clickbait and all-capital letters.
  • Preview text: the short line shown after the subject. Use it to add a second reason to open.
  • Opening line: get to the point quickly and speak to one person, not "dear customers".
  • One main call to action: a clear button such as "Shop the new collection" or "Download the guide".
  • Mobile first: most people read on their phones, so keep paragraphs short and buttons large.
  • Language: if your customers mainly speak Arabic, write in Arabic and check that your template displays right-to-left text correctly. If you serve both audiences, consider separate Arabic and English segments instead of mixing languages in one email.

Deliverability: make sure your emails reach the inbox

An email that lands in spam is an email nobody reads. Protect your sender reputation with a few habits:

  1. Authenticate your domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and send from your own domain.
  2. Email only people who opted in, and make unsubscribing easy.
  3. Remove addresses that bounce, and clean out subscribers who have not opened anything for a long time, after trying a short re-engagement email.
  4. Avoid sudden huge sends from a new account; grow your sending volume gradually.
  5. Keep a healthy balance of text and images, and avoid misleading subject lines.

Measure what matters

Email tools report many numbers. Focus on a few and compare them over time rather than chasing someone else's benchmark:

  • Click rate: how many people clicked a link. It tells you whether the content and offer were interesting.
  • Orders or revenue from email: use tracking links (UTM tags) so your store analytics can attribute sales to each campaign.
  • Unsubscribes and spam complaints: a sudden rise means you are sending too often or the content does not match what people signed up for.
  • List growth: how many new subscribers you add each month, and from where.

Open rates are less reliable than they used to be, because some email apps load images automatically and report opens that may not be real. Treat them as a rough signal, not a final verdict.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a small business send marketing emails?

Choose a rhythm you can keep consistently, such as once a week or every two weeks, and tell subscribers what to expect when they sign up. During big seasons you can send more often, as long as each email has a clear reason.

Is email marketing still worth it if my customers use WhatsApp?

Yes. WhatsApp is great for quick conversations and order updates, while email is better for longer content, product catalogues, guides and automated sequences. Many businesses use both: WhatsApp for one-to-one chats and email for campaigns and nurturing.

Can I add my existing customers to my email list?

Only if they agreed to receive marketing emails, or if the rules in your country allow it for existing customers. When in doubt, invite them to subscribe with a clear opt-in rather than adding them automatically.

Should I write my emails in Arabic or English?

Write in the language your customers prefer. If your audience is mixed, ask for their preferred language on the signup form and send separate versions, and make sure your Arabic emails display correctly from right to left.

What is a good first lead magnet?

Something small and genuinely useful for your ideal customer: a short guide, a checklist, a first-order discount or early access to a sale. It should be quick to consume and closely related to what you sell.
