How to Write Product Descriptions That Sell: A Guide for Online Stores

November 17, 20269 min readE-commerce

In a physical shop, a customer can pick up a product, feel the fabric, smell the perfume and ask the person behind the counter a question. Online, your product page has to do all of that work on its own. The photos show what the product looks like; the description explains why it is worth buying. Yet many online stores still use one generic line, or paste the supplier's text word for word, and then wonder why visitors leave without ordering.

This guide shows you how to write product descriptions that answer real questions and make the decision easier, with practical advice for stores selling in Arabic, English or both.

Start with the buyer, not the product

Before writing, picture the person most likely to buy this product. What are they trying to achieve? What worries them? Where will they use it? A description for a busy mother buying school bags reads very differently from one for a teenager choosing a backpack for style.

A quick way to get this right is to collect the questions customers already ask you on WhatsApp, Instagram comments or by phone. Every repeated question is a gap in your current product page. If people keep asking whether a dress runs small, the description should answer that before anyone has to ask.

Turn features into benefits

Features describe the product; benefits describe what changes for the customer. Both matter, but benefits are what make people care. A simple way to convert one into the other is to add "so that" after each feature.

FeatureBenefit
Double-wall stainless steel bottleKeeps water cold through a hot afternoon outdoors
Breathable cotton fabricStays comfortable in summer heat
Concentrated perfume oilA few drops last for hours, so the bottle lasts longer
Printable PDF plannerPlan your month on paper or on a tablet, whichever you prefer

Keep the specifications too: sizes, materials, dimensions, weight and what is in the box. Buyers who are close to a decision look for these details, and missing information is a common reason for returns.

A structure you can reuse

You do not need to reinvent every description. A consistent structure makes writing faster and helps customers scan:

  1. Opening line: who the product is for and the main benefit, in one or two sentences.
  2. Key benefits: three to five short points.
  3. Details and specifications: a short list or table.
  4. How to use or care for it: practical instructions.
  5. Delivery and returns: a short line with a link to your policy.
  6. Questions: two or three common questions answered directly.

For low-priced, familiar products, a short version is enough. For expensive or unfamiliar products, longer descriptions that address doubts usually help, because the customer needs more reassurance before paying.

Write in your brand voice

A luxury perfume house, a playful children's brand and a professional tools store should not sound the same. Decide on a few words that describe your voice, such as "warm and confident" or "simple and practical", and keep a short page of example descriptions you like. Consistent tone across your store builds trust and makes your brand recognisable.

Avoid empty phrases that every store uses, such as "high quality" or "best product", unless you immediately show what makes it so. Specific details are more convincing than adjectives.

Arabic and English pages

Many stores in the Gulf sell to both Arabic and English speakers. A literal translation rarely works well in either direction. Instead:

  • Write each language for its reader, keeping the same facts but adapting phrasing and examples.
  • Use the terms your customers actually search for in each language, including common spellings and dialect words in Arabic.
  • Check that Arabic pages display right-to-left correctly, including numbers, prices and units.
  • Have a native speaker review important pages before publishing.

Help shoppers find the product

Good descriptions also help search engines and your store's own search understand what you sell. Use the words customers would type in the product title and first lines, naturally rather than repeated over and over. Write an original description instead of copying the supplier's text, which may already appear on many other sites. Add clear image file names and alt text, and answer common questions on the page itself. These basics help without any technical knowledge.

Be honest and specific

Overpromising sells once and costs you later through returns, bad reviews and lost trust. Describe colours, sizes and results accurately, and say clearly what the product does not do if customers often assume it does. Do not invent reviews, numbers of customers or health claims. If a product needs a disclaimer, such as fragrance notes varying slightly or results differing between people, include it calmly and clearly.

Using AI to write faster

AI assistants can draft descriptions in seconds, but the output is only as good as the information you provide. Give the tool the facts about the product, your target buyer, your brand voice and two or three example descriptions you like, then edit the draft yourself. Check every fact, remove generic phrases and make sure nothing is promised that the product cannot deliver.

Improve with real feedback

Treat product descriptions as living content. Read your reviews and customer questions every month and update the pages they relate to. If a product has many visitors but few orders, try rewriting the opening line and benefits, improving the photos, or adding missing details. Change one thing at a time and give it enough visitors before you judge the result.

Start with your bestsellers: pick your five most visited products, list the questions customers ask about each, and rewrite their descriptions using the structure above. If you sell seasonal products, plan those pages early, and for ready-made planning tools see our marketing templates.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a product description be?

It depends on price and familiarity. Simple, low-cost products may need only a short paragraph and a few points, while expensive or unfamiliar products benefit from longer descriptions that answer doubts. Always make the key benefit and important details easy to find.

Can I use the supplier's product description?

You can use it as a source of facts, but rewrite it in your own words and voice. Copied text is often already on many other sites, says nothing about why customers should buy from you, and may not match your audience.

Should I write separate Arabic and English descriptions?

Yes, ideally. Keep the facts the same but write each version for its reader, using the words customers search for in that language, and have a native speaker review important pages.

Can AI write my product descriptions?

AI can produce a useful first draft quickly if you give it accurate product facts, your target buyer and examples of your brand voice. Always edit the result, check every claim and remove generic phrases before publishing.

How do I know if a description is working?

Look at how many visitors to the product page go on to buy, along with returns and customer questions about that product. If visits are high but orders are low, test a new opening line, clearer benefits or better photos, one change at a time.
