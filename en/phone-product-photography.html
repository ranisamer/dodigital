Product Photography with Your Phone: A Practical Guide for Online Stores

November 21, 20269 min readE-commerce

In an online store, the photo is the product. Customers cannot touch the fabric, smell the perfume or turn the box in their hands, so they decide based on what they see. Blurry, dark or inconsistent photos make even a great product look cheap, while clear and honest photos build the trust that leads to a purchase.

The good news is that you do not need a professional camera or a studio. Modern smartphones are more than capable of taking good product photos for a small store. What makes the difference is light, background, setup and consistency. This guide walks you through a simple, low-cost process you can repeat for every new product.

Start with light, not the camera

Light matters more than any phone setting. The easiest option is natural daylight from a window:

  • Place your table next to a large window, with the light coming from the side.
  • Avoid direct, harsh sun. If the light is strong, hang a thin white curtain or baking paper over the window to soften it.
  • Turn off ceiling lights, because mixing warm indoor lights with daylight creates odd colours.
  • Use a white card or foam board on the opposite side of the window to bounce light back and soften shadows.

In the Gulf, daylight is strong for most of the day, so the softer morning or late afternoon light is often easier to work with. If you shoot in the evening, one or two affordable LED panels or a ring light with a diffuser can give you steady, repeatable light.

Choose a simple background

For main product photos, a plain white or light-grey background keeps the attention on the product and works well on most store platforms and marketplaces. A cheap way to create one is a "sweep": a large sheet of white card or paper curved from the wall down onto the table, so there is no visible line behind the product.

For lifestyle photos, use backgrounds that suit your brand and customer: a wooden table for handmade goods, marble for jewellery or cosmetics, fabric for gifts. Keep props simple and make sure they never compete with the product itself.

Before every session, wipe your phone's lens. A fingerprint on the lens is one of the most common reasons for hazy photos.

Phone settings that make a difference

  • Use the main lens rather than the ultra-wide one, which can distort the shape of products. Step back slightly and zoom a little, or use the phone's 2x option if it has one, to keep proportions natural.
  • Tap to focus on the product, then adjust exposure by sliding your finger so whites look white, not grey.
  • Turn off the flash. It creates harsh reflections and flat light.
  • Use the timer with a tripod so the phone does not shake when you tap the shutter.
  • Shoot at the highest quality your phone offers, then resize later for your store.

The shots every product needs

Think about the questions a customer would ask if they were holding the product in a shop, then answer them with photos:

  1. Main photo: the full product, front view, on a clean background.
  2. Different angles: side, back and top, as relevant.
  3. Details: close-ups of texture, stitching, materials, labels or ingredients.
  4. Scale: the product in a hand, on a person or next to a familiar object, so the size is clear.
  5. In use: a lifestyle photo showing the product in a real setting.
  6. What's in the box: everything the customer will receive.

Good photos and good words work together. Once your images are ready, our guide to writing product descriptions that sell helps you write the text around them.

Light editing, honest results

Free editing apps on your phone are enough for most stores. Keep edits simple: straighten and crop, adjust brightness and contrast, correct the white balance so whites look neutral, and remove small dust spots. Avoid heavy filters and never change the colour or shape of the product. If the customer receives something that looks different from the photo, you can expect returns and disappointed reviews.

Some store owners use AI tools to remove backgrounds or generate lifestyle scenes. If you do, check the result carefully and make sure the product itself still looks exactly as it is in real life.

Consistency makes your store look professional

A store where every product photo has the same background, light and framing looks organised and trustworthy. Write down your setup once: the table position, the distance from the window, the tripod height, the angle and the crop ratio, such as square or 4:5. Then reuse it for every product. Batch your shoots too: photograph several products in one session while the light and setup stay the same.

Name your image files clearly, for example with the product name, and add descriptive alt text when you upload them. This helps accessibility and can support how your products appear in search.

Photos for social media and ads

The same session can supply content for your social media. While your setup is ready, take extra photos in vertical format, short clips of the product being opened or used, and close-ups that work well as story backgrounds. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Shooting under yellow ceiling lights, which changes product colours.
  • Busy backgrounds and props that distract from the product.
  • Using the manufacturer's or other sellers' photos without permission.
  • Only one photo per product, leaving customers with unanswered questions.
  • Heavy filters that make the product look different from reality.
  • Different styles for every product, which makes the store look messy.
Try this today: set up a table by a window with a white card sweep, wipe your lens, and photograph your best-selling product from six angles. Edit lightly, upload the new photos, and use the same setup for the rest of your catalogue over the coming weeks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a professional camera for product photos?

Not for most small stores. A recent smartphone, good light and a simple background are enough to take clear, attractive product photos. A professional camera helps mainly with very detailed or technical products.

What is the best background for product photos?

A plain white or light-grey background works well for main photos because it keeps attention on the product. Use more styled backgrounds for lifestyle photos that show the product in use.

How many photos should each product have?

There is no fixed number, but most products benefit from a main photo, several angles, close-up details, a photo showing scale and at least one lifestyle photo.

Can I use the supplier's product photos?

Only if you have clear permission to use them. Your own photos also help your store stand out from other sellers offering the same product.

How do I photograph shiny products like jewellery or glass?

Use soft, diffused light, avoid the flash, and place white cards around the product to control reflections. Small changes in angle can remove distracting reflections.
